# Mosaic: Building a Live Design Editor So I Could Stop Guessing Pixels

One of the more interesting side-problems I ran into building Mosaic, my personal activity-discovery app, was one I didn't expect: how do you actually tune the visual composition of a hero section — the phone mockup and three floating "capture cards" scattered around it — when every adjustment means editing a percentage in code, rebuilding, and looking at a screenshot to see if it worked?

Like any product decision, I started with the problem rather than the solution. The hero section had a phone mockup with three cards (a pottery workshop, a breathwork session, a farmers market) positioned around it using plain CSS `top`/`left` percentages and a rotation value, hardcoded in the component. Getting the composition to feel intentional — enough breathing room, no crowding, no overlap with the phone's own on-screen content — meant guessing a number, saving, reloading the page, and eyeballing it. When that got tedious enough, I tried offloading it: I wrote up the whole component, the design tokens, and a plain-English description of what I wanted, and handed it to ChatGPT to suggest better coordinates. It could reason about spacing in the abstract, but it was guessing blind — it had never seen the actual rendered page, so its suggested percentages were no more reliable than my own guesses, just dressed up with more confident reasoning.

That's what convinced me the fix wasn't a smarter way to ask for coordinates, it was removing the guessing step entirely. So I built a live design mode instead: a floating toggle that turns any positioned element into something you can drag, resize, and rotate directly in the browser, and turns any headline or button label into text you click into and just type. Drag it, see it, it's already saved — that's the whole loop, no code-edit-screenshot cycle in between.

The part I think is actually clever isn't the drag interaction itself, it's what it's connected to. Both the position data and the copy live in two small JSON files that the component already imports as its source of truth. The design mode overlay doesn't maintain some separate preview state — it posts straight to those same files on disk (through a tiny Vite dev plugin with zero new dependencies, just Node's own file system module), and the page re-renders from the updated JSON automatically. Editing visually and editing the source file are the same action. There's no "apply changes" step, no export, nothing to get out of sync.

A couple of the underlying decisions mattered more than I expected once I started using it. Resizing a card scales it uniformly rather than stretching its width and letting the text reflow — the cards are fixed little compositions of a photo, a source label, and a title, and reflowing them independently would have broken that composition the way resizing a photo by dragging just one edge does. And once the phone mockup itself became a draggable, absolutely-positioned element, the container around it had nothing left in normal document flow to give it height — instead of hand-guessing a minimum height and having it drift out of sync every time I changed something, I marked the phone as the "anchor" and had it report its own real rendered height back up automatically. Small thing, but it's the difference between the section quietly growing a gap underneath it and just working.

The text-editing side had its own gotcha worth remembering: making something `contentEditable` and syncing React state on every keystroke sounds reasonable until you try it — the re-render replaces the DOM node out from under your own cursor, and the caret jumps to the wrong place after every character. Committing the value only on blur, and letting the DOM own the text while you're actively typing, sidesteps that completely. It's a small implementation detail, but it's exactly the kind of thing that's invisible when it works and infuriating when it doesn't.

Working with AI on this meant I never had to write the drag math, the resize-observer wiring, or the file-watching plumbing myself — which meant the actual time I spent went into the part that's genuinely mine to decide: does this composition feel right, is there too much rotation on that card, should the breathwork card sit lower. That's the trade I keep making across Mosaic generally — AI handles the mechanism, I spend my attention on the judgment call the mechanism exists to serve.
